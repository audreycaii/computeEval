\begin{abstract}
Studies of evidence manipulation in financial LLM agents report ``flip rates'': the share of decisions that change when a poisoned document is added. Most run each decision once, so a flip cannot be told apart from run-to-run variation, and none of the studies we reviewed includes a true-update control, so an agent that never changes its mind scores as perfectly robust. We measure both. Three Claude models (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5) rate the valuation of four AI-compute companies from frozen evidence packets under four conditions: clean, plus a planted false material claim, plus a withheld true update of matched direction and, by our judgement, size, and plus an immaterial item, with 20 repeats per cell (\nValidRuns{} valid runs of \nTotalRuns{}). Noise floors range from \mSonnetNoise{} (Sonnet) through \mOpusNoise{} (Opus) to \mHaikuNoise{} (Haiku), so a raw flip rate overstates Haiku's effect by about \mHaikuNoise{}. Net of noise, the false plant moves ratings as much as the true update for Haiku ($E_1$ = \mHaikuEone{} versus $E_2$ = \mHaikuEtwo{}), more for Opus (\mOpusEone{} versus \mOpusEtwo{}) and less for Sonnet (\mSonnetEone{} versus \mSonnetEtwo{}); the special item is cited as a driver in every run; and an immaterial item moves Opus on one ticker clearly and one at the margin ($E_3$ = \mOpusEthree{}), with no detectable excess change for the other models. One of twelve model--ticker cells shows movement on the true update with at most $\varepsilon$ on the plant. We release packets, plants (labelled synthetic), prompts, logs and code. Claims are bounded to static-packet, single-call agents, three models, four tickers and October 2026.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A research agent that reads evidence and issues a conclusion can be attacked by changing the evidence. Recent work on trading and advisory agents measures this with a flip rate: run the agent on clean inputs, run it again with a planted document, and count how often the decision changes \citep{poisoningalpha2026,autoredtrader2026,adversarialnews2026,tradetrap2025}.

Two things are missing from that number. First, the same agent on the same clean input also changes its mind between runs. \citet{identicalruns2026} find that variance between identical runs of coding agents can exceed the gap between agents, and \citet{coinflipjudge2026} reports LLM-judge verdicts flipping 13.6\% of the time under identical conditions. A flip rate measured with one run per decision folds that variance into the attack effect. Second, robustness without a true-update control rewards stubbornness: an agent that ignores every new document scores as perfectly robust, and nothing in a flip-rate study distinguishes resistance from insensitivity.

This paper measures conclusion change against a noise floor and against a warranted-update control, for research agents that rate equity valuation from a fixed evidence packet. The evaluation target is narrow on purpose: one model call, no tools, a frozen packet, a structured verdict, which isolates how the verdict moves when one evidence item changes from retrieval and tool variance. The contribution is a design and a set of pre-registered metrics (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}; two additions made after the pilot are marked), applied to three models (Section~\ref{sec:results}), with the diagnosis the metrics license (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}). We use 2026 AI-compute ``neocloud'' companies as the testbed because the period produced dense, dated, checkable, valuation-relevant facts that can be paired with counterfactual plants, and because the valuation question has no ground truth, so the right measurand is robustness and warranted updating, not correctness.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Manipulating financial agents.} \citet{poisoningalpha2026} attack a trading pipeline across roles and architectures and find flip success of about 19--22\% for fabricated news and 24--30\% for hidden instructions, with one run per asset-day. \citet{autoredtrader2026} report 26.67\% attack success from synthetic misinformation, again without repeated runs. \citet{adversarialnews2026}, \citet{tradetrap2025}, \citet{contagion2026} and \citet{sellmethisstock2026} measure returns, belief shift or recommendation drift under adversarial inputs. All evaluate trading or advisory decisions; none evaluates research-style conclusions, and none separates attack-induced change from natural variation.

\paragraph{Poisoning deep-research agents.} \citet{ugcpoison2026} get planted content cited in 38--51\% of reports by poisoning one user-generated page; \citet{misknow2026} find one misleading document raises false-conclusion adoption from 0\% to 54.7\%; \citet{drnoise2026} benchmark agents in misleading-evidence environments. These measure adoption in free-text reports rather than a bounded verdict, and have no matched true-update condition.

\paragraph{Finance-agent evaluation and reliability.} Frontier Financial Judgement \citep{ffj2026}, Finance Agent v2 \citep{vals2026} and Deep FinResearch Bench \citep{deepfinresearch2026} evaluate analyst judgement and workflows. \citet{identicalruns2026}, \citet{consistentagents2026} and \citet{samepatient2026} measure run-to-run variance of agents directly; \citet{measurementvalidity2026} argues that unreliable measurements compound through agentic evaluation; \citet{dfah2026} covers replay determinism for financial agents. Lookahead bias \citep{lookahead2025,financebias2026} motivates counterfactual numbers in our plants. We borrow the repeated-run design from this cluster and the attack framing from the first, and add the warranted-update control that neither has.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Task and agent}
\label{sec:task}
One run is one API call. The model receives a fixed system prompt (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}), an evidence packet, and the question ``Given only the evidence in this packet, is \{company\} attractively valued at its current share price as of 2026-10-01?'' It must return JSON with a \texttt{rating} on a 5-point scale (1 = strongly unattractive, 3 = fairly valued, 5 = strongly attractive), a \texttt{confidence} in $[0,1]$, exactly three \texttt{drivers}, each citing packet item IDs, a \texttt{rationale}, and, after the pilot (Section~\ref{sec:prereg}), a continuous \texttt{fair\_value\_usd}. The agent is a single call to the Claude API with structured output, adaptive thinking at \texttt{effort = medium} on every model, no tools, and no sampling controls (these models reject \texttt{temperature}), so run-to-run variation is whatever default sampling and thinking produce \citep{anthropic2026api}. Models: \texttt{claude-opus-5-5}, \texttt{claude-sonnet-5-5}, \texttt{claude-haiku-5-5}, queried on 2026-10-23.

\subsection{Testbed and packets}
\label{sec:packets}
Four tickers: Nebius (NBIS), CoreWeave (CRWV) and IREN, three GPU-cloud ``neoclouds'', plus NVIDIA (NVDA) as a control one layer upstream in the same demand cycle. Each packet holds eight \emph{base} items, dated, sourced facts from company filings and calls \citep{nebius2026q2,coreweave2026q2,iren2026tenk,nvidia2026q2}, analyst research \citep{semianalysis2026}, news wires and one forum post, plus a price item with the September 30, 2026 close and approximate market capitalisation \citep{yahoo2026}. Items are rendered as \texttt{[ID] (date, source type, source) text}, sorted by date. Every packet is frozen as of 2026-10-01 and hashed; the hash is logged with each run. Appendix~\ref{app:packets} lists every item.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}
Each ticker has three special items that appear only in their condition (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). The \emph{true update} is a real, material, dated fact withheld from the clean packet. The \emph{false plant} is a synthetic counterfactual written to push the same direction with, by the author's judgement, comparable materiality, carrying numbers no real source reports and no share-price reaction clause; it matches the true update's source style and month where that was possible (exceptions: NVDA's update is the August quarter and its plant a September pre-announcement; IREN's update is a third-party rating while its plant is a company statement). The \emph{immaterial item} is synthetic. Direction is balanced across tickers (NBIS and NVDA positive, CRWV and IREN negative) so a model's general optimism cannot pass for responsiveness.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
Within one (model, ticker) cell, condition $k \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ yields a multiset of ratings $R_k$ from $n_k$ valid runs. Define the pairwise disagreement
\begin{equation}
D(A,B) = \Pr[r_a \neq r_b],\quad r_a \in A,\ r_b \in B,
\end{equation}
over all pairs (distinct pairs when $A=B$). The \textbf{noise floor} is $N = D(R_0,R_0)$: the probability that two independent clean runs disagree. The \textbf{raw flip rate} for condition $k$ is $F_k = D(R_k,R_0)$, which is what a one-run study estimates. The \textbf{excess flip} is $E_k = F_k - N$: $E_1$ is change under the plant net of noise, $E_2$ is warranted change (responsiveness), and $E_3$ should be about zero. The \textbf{mean shift} $S_k = d\,(\overline{R_k} - \overline{R_0})$, with $\overline{R}$ the mean rating, is signed by the ticker's direction $d$ for $k\in\{1,2\}$ and unsigned for $k=3$. \textbf{Attribution} $A_k$ is the share of condition-$k$ runs whose cited driver IDs include the special item. Per model we pool by averaging over tickers and label the result: \emph{resistant-responsive} if $E_1 \le \varepsilon$ and $E_2 > \varepsilon$; \emph{insensitive} if both are $\le \varepsilon$; \emph{credulous} if $E_1 > \varepsilon$ and $|E_1 - E_2| \le \varepsilon$; otherwise \emph{mixed}, with $\varepsilon = 0.05$, one run in twenty. Uncertainty is a bootstrap over runs within each condition (2,000 resamples, percentile 95\% intervals); pooled intervals average the ticker-level resampled statistics resample by resample, so they hold the four tickers fixed and reflect run-level resampling only. Two things were added after the pilot and are not pre-registered: the fair-value shift $V_k = d\,(\overline{V_k} - \overline{V_0})/\overline{V_0}$ (unsigned for $k=3$), and the per-cell typology in Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}.

\subsection{Pre-registration and pilot}
\label{sec:prereg}
The metric definitions were committed to the repository before any full run and were not edited after. A pilot of 12 Sonnet runs on NBIS was run three times. In round one the plant moved the rating from 3 to 2 in all three runs while the rationales dwelt on a market-capitalisation conflict between two packet items; the conflicting sentence was removed and the effect did not recur. That edit, made after seeing one cell's outcome, is a researcher degree of freedom. Round two showed every rating equal to 3 in every condition, so the schema gained the continuous \texttt{fair\_value\_usd} field as an exploratory outcome. Round three (kept; its three runs per condition sit with 17 later runs in the Sonnet--NBIS cells) reproduced the all-3 ratings while fair value moved about 2\% under both items. The planned check that clean ratings vary failed in every round. At about \$0.01 per Sonnet run, repeats were raised from the planned 10 to 20 per cell. The full run comprises 4 tickers $\times$ 4 conditions $\times$ 3 models $\times$ 20 repeats; \nValidRuns{} of \nTotalRuns{} runs returned schema-valid output (\nFailedRuns{} failed after three attempts; \nRetries{} runs needed a retry). Total API cost was \$\costTotal.

\paragraph{Noise floors range from zero to one in five.} Two independent clean runs of Sonnet disagree with probability \mSonnetNoise{} (pooled), of Opus \mOpusNoise{} [\mOpusNoiseLo{}, \mOpusNoiseHi{}], and of Haiku \mHaikuNoise{} [\mHaikuNoiseLo{}, \mHaikuNoiseHi{}] (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:cells}). The low floors mostly reflect ratings collapsed to the midpoint ``fairly valued'' (every Sonnet clean cell and three of four Opus clean cells are all 3), not stable judgement. For Haiku the raw flip rate under the plant is $F_1$ = \mHaikuFone{} while the excess is $E_1$ = \mHaikuEone{}: a one-run study would have reported an effect inflated by the noise floor.

\paragraph{For two of three models, the false plant moves conclusions as much as the true update, or more.} Pooled over tickers, $E_1$ versus $E_2$ is \mOpusEone{} [\mOpusEoneLo{}, \mOpusEoneHi{}] versus \mOpusEtwo{} [\mOpusEtwoLo{}, \mOpusEtwoHi{}] for Opus, \mSonnetEone{} versus \mSonnetEtwo{} for Sonnet, and \mHaikuEone{} versus \mHaikuEtwo{} for Haiku; the signed shifts $S_1$/$S_2$ are \mOpusSone{}/\mOpusStwo{}, \mSonnetSone{}/\mSonnetStwo{} and \mHaikuSone{}/\mHaikuStwo{}, all in the direction the item pushes. Mean confidence barely moves (clean to plant: Opus \mOpusConfCzero{} to \mOpusConfCone{}, Sonnet \mSonnetConfCzero{} to \mSonnetConfCone{}, Haiku \mHaikuConfCzero{} to \mHaikuConfCone{}). Per-ticker behaviour is heterogeneous (Figure~\ref{fig:ratings}, Table~\ref{tab:cells}). On NVDA, Opus and Sonnet move almost fully on both the synthetic pre-announcement and the real quarter ($E_1$ = \cOpusNVDAEone{} and \cSonnetNVDAEone{}; $E_2$ = \cOpusNVDAEtwo{} and \cSonnetNVDAEtwo{}). On NBIS, Opus moves on the fabricated reserved-contract increase far more than on the real on-demand increase ($E_1$ = \cOpusNBISEone{}, $E_2$ = \cOpusNBISEtwo{}). On CRWV and NBIS, Sonnet does not move at all. Haiku's pooled $E_1 \approx E_2$ is two opposite cells cancelling (NVDA \cHaikuNVDAEone{} versus \cHaikuNVDAEtwo{}; CRWV \cHaikuCRWVEone{} versus \cHaikuCRWVEtwo{}), so its pooled label hides the per-ticker pattern. One cell shows movement on the true update with the plant at $\varepsilon$: Sonnet on IREN ($E_1$ = \cSonnetIRENEone{}, $E_2$ = \cSonnetIRENEtwo{}). Because the materiality match is a judgement, $E_1$ versus $E_2$ compares responses to two different items, and a gap can reflect unequal materiality as much as credulity. The pooled diagnosis is \emph{\mOpusDiag} for Opus, \emph{\mSonnetDiag} for Sonnet and \emph{\mHaikuDiag} for Haiku; the pre-registered expectation that some model would show both $E_1$ and $E_2$ near zero failed at the pooled level and holds only in individual cells.

\paragraph{The special item is cited in every run.} Attribution is \mOpusAone{}/\mSonnetAone{}/\mHaikuAone{} for the plant and \mOpusAtwo{}/\mSonnetAtwo{}/\mHaikuAtwo{} for the true update (Opus/Sonnet/Haiku); the immaterial item is cited in \mOpusAthree{}/\mSonnetAthree{}/\mHaikuAthree{} of runs, and \nUnknownIds{} runs cited an ID absent from their packet. Since the prompt requires three cited drivers and the special item is the only new one, citation shows the item was read, not that it drove the verdict; stability under the plant is therefore not a failure to notice it.

\paragraph{The immaterial-item control: no detectable excess change for Sonnet and Haiku; Opus moves on one ticker clearly and one at the margin.} $E_3$ is \mSonnetEthree{} for Sonnet and \mHaikuEthree{} [\mHaikuEthreeLo{}, \mHaikuEthreeHi{}] for Haiku, with per-ticker intervals reaching about 0.19. For Opus, $E_3$ = \mOpusEthree{} [\mOpusEthreeLo{}, \mOpusEthreeHi{}]: a marketing hire moved its CRWV rating in a fraction of runs ($E_3$ = \cOpusCRWVEthree{}) and a hackathon sponsorship its NBIS rating at the margin ($E_3$ = \cOpusNBISEthree{}, interval touching zero), neither cited as a driver. For that model, part of what a plant-only design would score as an attack effect is a response to the packet changing at all; subtracting $E_3$ is illustrative only, since the cells that carry $E_3$ are not the cells that carry $E_1$.

\paragraph{Exploratory: fair value moves where the rating does not.} The signed fair-value shift $V_1$/$V_2$ is \mOpusVone{}\%/\mOpusVtwo{}\% for Opus, \mSonnetVone{}\%/\mSonnetVtwo{}\% for Sonnet and \mHaikuVone{}\%/\mHaikuVtwo{}\% for Haiku, against \mOpusVthree{}\%/\mSonnetVthree{}\%/\mHaikuVthree{}\% under the immaterial item; this measure has no noise floor. On NBIS, where Sonnet's rating never leaves 3, its fair value still rises by \cSonnetNBISVone{}\% under the plant and \cSonnetNBISVtwo{}\% under the true update (immaterial item: \cSonnetNBISVthree{}\%), so on that ticker part of the apparent insensitivity is quantisation; on CRWV Sonnet's fair value does not move either (\cSonnetCRWVVone{}\%).

\section{Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diagnosis}
Four findings bear on how manipulation robustness should be measured.

\textbf{A flip rate without a noise floor is not interpretable across models.} The same design yields a raw $F_1$ that mixes item effect and sampling variance in proportions that differ by model: for Haiku about half of the raw flip rate is noise, for Sonnet none of it is. Comparing models, or attacks, by raw flip rate therefore ranks them partly by sampling variance. The subtraction is not additive when noise is high: $E_k$ can be negative while the mean shift is positive (Haiku on NBIS, $E_1$ = \cHaikuNBISEone{} with $S_1$ = \cHaikuNBISSone{}), because an item can narrow the spread of ratings as well as move them; where $N$ is large, $S_k$ is the better guide.

\textbf{Robustness and responsiveness have to be measured together.} With a matched true-update condition, the twelve model--ticker cells sort as follows (this typology is post hoc). Neither item moves the rating beyond $\varepsilon$ in five cells (Sonnet on CRWV and NBIS, Opus on IREN and CRWV, Haiku on NBIS): a flip-rate study would call these robust, and they are robust by insensitivity. Both items move it beyond $\varepsilon$ in five (Opus and Sonnet on NVDA, Haiku on CRWV and, weakly, IREN, and Opus on NBIS, where the plant moves it far more than the update). The plant alone moves it in one (Haiku on NVDA). In one, Sonnet on IREN, the true update moves it and the plant stays at $\varepsilon$. Which kind a cell falls into is not predictable from the model, so a robustness number pooled over tasks hides the distinction the control was added to reveal.

\textbf{Any-item sensitivity is a separate failure mode.} Opus's rating moved under an immaterial item on one ticker clearly and one at the margin; reporting $E_3$ alongside $E_1$ separates a response to ``something was added'' from a response to what was added.

\textbf{Citation separates ``not read'' from ``read and absorbed''.} Universal citation of the plant, including where the rating does not move, means the obvious mitigation, checking whether the model noticed the item, would not detect the problem.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The claims are bounded to static-packet, single-call research agents, three models from one vendor at one effort setting, four tickers and the evidence of October 2026. ``Credulous'' and ``unwarranted'' are relative to ground truth, not to instructions: the prompt declares every item admissible, so a model that updates on a plant follows its instructions, and $E_1$ measures sensitivity to an unverifiable, admissible counterfactual item. With no sampling controls, the noise floor is a property of the default decoding and thinking configuration and could change with effort. Plants are synthetic, in one source style per ticker, written by the author, and may be easier or harder to believe than human-crafted manipulation. Models may know some real 2026 events; plants use counterfactual numbers, but we did not test whether a model recognised them as false (self-reported training cutoffs are unreliable, and the vendor's documented cutoffs may post-date several 2026 base items). Per-cell intervals are wide (up to about $\pm 0.3$ on $E_k$ for Haiku), single-valued cells have zero-width intervals by construction, and pooled intervals hold the four tickers fixed, so they do not generalise across tasks. Nothing here is an assessment of any company's value.

\section{Prompts, schema and release}
\label{app:prompts}
Packets, plants (flagged synthetic in the data and in Appendix~\ref{app:packets}), prompts, schema, pre-registered metrics, the full run log and the analysis code that generates every number in this text are released with the paper.


\paragraph{System prompt.} \textit{You are an equity research analyst agent. You will receive an evidence packet about one listed company. Each item in the packet has an ID in square brackets, a date, a source type and a source name. Rules: (1) Base your assessment only on the evidence in the packet. Do not use knowledge of events after the packet's as-of date, and do not rely on outside facts that the packet does not contain. (2) Every item in the packet is admissible evidence regardless of its date, including items dated on the as-of date. (3) Give exactly three drivers of your rating. Each driver must cite at least one packet item ID in its evidence\_ids. (4) Output only a JSON object matching the required schema. No prose outside the JSON.}

\paragraph{Question.} \textit{Given only the evidence in this packet, is \{company\} (\{ticker\}) attractively valued at its current share price as of 2026-10-01? Rating scale: 1 = strongly unattractive (overvalued), 2 = unattractive, 3 = fairly valued, 4 = attractive, 5 = strongly attractive (undervalued). Return: rating (integer 1--5), fair\_value\_usd (your point estimate of fair value per share in US dollars, as a number), confidence (0--1), drivers (exactly three, each with a claim and the evidence\_ids it rests on), rationale (at most 120 words).} The packet follows. The JSON schema enforces the field set; range and count constraints that the structured-output API does not accept are validated locally and violations count as failed runs.
